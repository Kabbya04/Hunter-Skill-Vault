# Design Research and Current Resources

Use live research when current facts affect the result. Design patterns, platform guidance, browser support, font availability, icon libraries, and trend language change; verify them rather than relying on memory.

## Source order

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Official platform, standards, browser, framework, and library documentation.
2. Original work from designers, studios, foundries, artists, and product teams.
3. Maintained repositories, release notes, case studies, and conference talks.
4. Reputable publications and curated showcases.
5. Social posts and trend roundups only as leads, not authority.

For accessibility, use current WCAG/WAI material and platform accessibility documentation. For browser support, check current compatibility data. For fonts and assets, verify license terms from the publisher.

## Research queries

Search for decisions, not vague inspiration:

- `[domain] workflow interface case study`
- `[audience] accessibility needs [task]`
- `[platform] current design guidelines [pattern]`
- `[visual lineage] typography color composition`
- `[technique] browser support accessibility performance`
- `[font name] language support variable axes license`
- `[icon library] license tree shaking accessibility`
- `[trend] usability critique accessibility`

Add the current year only when recency matters. A historical reference does not become better by being recent.

## Build a reference matrix

For each source, capture:

- Source and date accessed.
- Why it is credible or useful.
- Principle extracted.
- Element influenced: hierarchy, type, color, layout, icon, imagery, copy, or motion.
- What must not be copied.

Keep the set small enough to reason about. Five strong, varied references are more useful than fifty screenshots.

## Trend evaluation

Evaluate any trend against:

1. **Fit:** Does it express the design thesis?
2. **Function:** Does it improve comprehension, navigation, feedback, or emotion?
3. **Content:** Does it work with realistic volume and edge cases?
4. **Access:** Does it survive contrast, zoom, keyboard use, and reduced motion?
5. **Performance:** Is its cost proportionate to the value?
6. **Durability:** Will the core experience remain coherent after the novelty fades?
7. **Fallback:** Does it degrade safely on unsupported devices?

Reject a trend that fails fit or function. Adapt one that only needs accessibility or fallback corrections.

## Synthesis rules

- Borrow principles and techniques, not compositions or identifiable visual signatures.
- Combine references from different categories and lineages.
- Preserve domain truth; do not style away important conventions.
- Cite direct inspiration when communicating the rationale.
- Do not reproduce proprietary assets, illustrations, icons, or copy.
- Check licenses before installing fonts, icon sets, images, shaders, or code.
- Distinguish observation (“many current examples use…”) from recommendation (“this product should use…”).

## Resource categories

Look for the best current source in each relevant category rather than relying on a permanent list of fashionable sites:

- Official platform and accessibility guidance.
- Browser standards and compatibility.
- Type foundries and font catalogs with explicit licensing.
- Museum, archive, architecture, editorial, packaging, and industrial references.
- Product teardowns and original design case studies.
- Award galleries and curated showcases for discovery.
- Motion studies with real interaction context.
- Open-source icon and component repositories.
- Domain-specific tools used by the actual audience.

Curated galleries reveal presentation trends but often hide usability, responsiveness, accessibility, and state design. Never treat a polished screenshot as proof that a pattern works.

## Reporting research

Summarize only what changed the design:

- The product/domain convention learned.
- The visual or behavioral principle adopted.
- The current capability or constraint verified.
- The trend rejected or adapted and why.

Include links and access dates when presenting external findings. Do not pad delivery with an inspiration dump.
